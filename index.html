A
Enrollment Basics
9 Q&A
01What is the Microsoft Cloud Partner Program?
Microsoft's official partner membership program — formerly the Microsoft Partner Network (MPN), now the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program. A global community of partners, offerings, and resources to build and deploy successful business solutions.
02How do I enroll and become a Microsoft partner?
Visit partner.microsoft.com → "Become a Partner." Steps: (1) Create a free account with org info and goals; (2) Confirm company details (address, legal contact, registration number); (3) Validate credentials (email, employment).
03How long does it take to get approved as a partner?
The sign-up form is quick, but verification may take a few business days to a couple of weeks. Microsoft verifies your email (domain ownership) and business details.
04Do I need to sign any agreement or contract?
Yes — the Microsoft Partner Agreement covering code of conduct, confidentiality, trademark usage, and policy compliance.
05What is a "Partner ID" (formerly MPN ID) and why is it important?
A unique identifier for your organization's partnership with Microsoft. Critical for Partner Admin Link (PAL), applying for programs, and tracking your capability score.
06Are there program levels like "Silver" and "Gold" in the new partner program?
No. The old Silver and Gold competency levels were retired in October 2022. They were replaced with the new Solutions Partner designations.
07What is Partner Center and what do I use it for?
The single destination to manage your business relationship with Microsoft — update company profile, add users, track designation progress, download software/license keys, manage referrals, sign up for incentive programs, and access support.
08Do I have to renew my partner membership each year?
Membership stays active while you remain in good standing — no annual reapplication. Paid benefits (Action Pack, Solutions Partner packages) require annual payment. Solutions Partner designations are reviewed yearly.
09What if my company has multiple branches or subsidiaries?
Use a Partner Global Account (PGA) as the primary account and add each country office or subsidiary as a separate Partner Location Account (PLA) underneath.
B
Partner Types & Business Models
7 Q&A
01What kinds of partners does Microsoft work with (ISV, SI, MSP, etc.)?
  • ISV — Builds/sells own software/SaaS on Azure, Teams, or Dynamics 365
  • SI — Delivers customer projects without reselling licenses
  • MSP — Provides ongoing monitoring, management, and support
  • VAR — Resells licenses + services (often via CSP)
  • CSP Partner — Sells cloud subscriptions. Direct bills Microsoft; Indirect via distributor
  • Advisory/Services-only — Consulting/deployment without reselling; recognized via PAL/CPOR
02Do I have to sell Microsoft products (be a reseller) to join the partner program?
No. Many partners only provide advisory or deployment services. Use PAL or CPOR to get credit. Associations are the sole mechanism by which points are acknowledged.
03What's the difference between a Microsoft partner and a Cloud Solution Provider (CSP)?
"Microsoft partner" is broad — any organization in the program. A CSP specifically resells Microsoft cloud subscriptions (Azure, M365, Dynamics 365) directly or via a distributor. All CSPs are partners, but not all partners are CSPs.
04We're an ISV. Is the Cloud Partner Program relevant to us?
Yes. Access technical resources, training, and support. List applications on the Microsoft commercial marketplace. Earn a Solutions Partner designation (e.g., Digital & App Innovation if built on Azure) to demonstrate cloud expertise.
05What if our company only offers services or consulting (no proprietary software)?
Many Microsoft partners are services-only. Pursue the relevant Solutions Partner designation and get credit for driving usage/deployments through partner association (PAL for Azure, CPOR for M365 projects). Being "services-only" does not limit your ability to succeed.
06What does it mean to be an "Advisory" partner?
Partners who advise customers without directly reselling. Use CPOR or PAL association methods so Microsoft knows you influenced those customer deployments.
07What's the difference between being a partner in MCPP vs being a distributor or LSP?
A Distributor (Indirect Provider) wholesales Microsoft products to other partners — very different track, high requirements. An LSP sells volume licenses under enterprise agreements. Unless your model is recruiting other resellers, you are likely a standard partner or CSP reseller.

Designations & Solution Areas
6 Q&A
01What is a "Solutions Partner" designation?
An earned credential showing proven capability and success in a specific Microsoft solution area. 6 solution areas: Data & AI (Azure), Digital & App Innovation (Azure), Infrastructure (Azure), Business Applications, Modern Work, Security.
02What happened to the old Gold and Silver competencies?
Retired in October 2022. Microsoft transitioned from the legacy Gold/Silver competency system to the new Solutions Partner designations based on a 100-point scoring model.
03Is there a designation just for being a general Microsoft partner?
No. The current program is structured around specific solution areas. If you don't yet qualify for a Solutions Partner designation, you remain a partner member without a specific designation.
04How do I know which Solution Area is right for my company?
  • Infrastructure (Azure) → Migrate/manage cloud resources
  • Digital & App Innovation → App development, modernization, DevOps
  • Data & AI (Azure) → Analytics, data warehousing, AI/ML on Azure
  • Business Applications → Dynamics 365 or Power Platform
  • Modern Work → Office 365, Teams, SharePoint, device management
  • Security → Cybersecurity (Defender, Sentinel, Entra, Purview)
05Do we need to choose a single solution area, or can we work in multiple?
Free to focus on one or multiple areas. Each designation is earned independently. Many partners start with one area and earn multiple designations over time.
06Can I change or add Solution Areas later?
Yes. You don't "pick" a solution area at signup — designations are something you work towards. Partner Center tracks progress separately for each area.
D
Achieving a Solutions Partner Designation
19 Q&A
01How do we become a Solutions Partner?
Accumulate a sufficient Partner Capability Score (PCS) for your chosen solution area. Max 100 points across 3 categories. Reach at least 70 points total with no category at 0 pts.
02What are the "Performance, Skilling, and Customer Success" criteria?
  • Performance — Net customer adds (new minus lost, trailing 12 months)
  • Skilling — Intermediate and Advanced Certifications held by your team
  • Customer Success — Usage growth and deployments across customer workloads
03What is the Partner Capability Score, and how is it calculated?
Microsoft's 100-point measure of a partner's readiness in each solution area. Calculated automatically from Partner Center data using defined thresholds. Partners must score at least 70 points with activity in all three areas.
04Do the requirements differ for different solution areas?
The general structure is consistent, but specific metrics and qualifying certifications differ by area. See the Scoring Matrix on the Overview tab for point breakdowns per area.
05Once I reach 70 points, do I automatically get the Solutions Partner designation?
Once you reach 70 PCS points with non-zero in every required metric, you attain Qualified status. Microsoft automatically enrolls your profile — no separate "apply" action required. However, you must meet the same criteria again during the annual renewal window.
06Do we have to meet all three criteria or can we skip one?
Cannot skip any category. You need at least some points in each category (every metric must be > 0). You don't need to max out all three.
07How can we track our Partner Capability Score?
Log into Partner Center → Membership section. View enrollment status, qualification status, and metric-level details for each solution area.
08What is the time frame to achieve a Solutions Partner designation?
Metrics measured on a rolling 12-month basis. For new partners, eligibility window includes the current month and the last five months. Most new partners take 6–18 months to achieve their first designation.
09What are "net customer adds" in the Performance category?
New customers started working with minus any lost customers (trailing 12 months). For Azure: a customer with at least $1,000 ACR/month counts as an add. Customers with ACR below $1,000 for two straight months count against the partner.
10What counts as customer "Usage Growth"?
Increasing consumption of Microsoft services. For Azure: ACR growth year-over-year (minimum $1,000 ACR). For Modern Work: active usage of M365 services. For Business Applications: Dynamics 365 monthly active usage.
11What are the "deployments" in the Customer Success category?
Specific Azure workloads or M365 features deployed for customers. For Infrastructure: needs 5 unique Azure Service Level 2 workloads (all Azure services eligible except virtual machines and VM licenses).
12Do we have to earn all three Azure designations or just one?
You can pursue any or all. Each covers different specialties. Some certifications and customer work can contribute to multiple Azure designations. Many cloud partners eventually achieve all three.
13Can I lose a Solutions Partner designation after achieving it?
Yes. Valid for one year until your anniversary/renewal date. If your PCS falls below 70 (e.g., certified employees leave or a major customer's usage declines), you won't meet the renewal criteria.
14If we achieve a designation in one area, does it help with others?
Some overlap exists — certain certifications count toward multiple areas. However, the score itself is separate per area — an 80-point score in Modern Work doesn't give you an Azure designation.
15Do all partners need to become Solutions Partners?
No. You can remain a member without a designation. However, earning a designation gives you a badge, is often a prerequisite for incentive programs and co-sell eligibility, and signals competence to customers.
16What if our company works primarily with small businesses?
Microsoft has separate Enterprise vs. SMB tracks in five of six solution areas. In Modern Work and Security, Microsoft evaluates you on both paths and chooses the highest score.
17Do I need to report my performance to Microsoft, or is it tracked automatically?
Tracked automatically as long as you have proper associations (PAL, CPOR, DPOR, CSP) in place. Without associations, points are not recognized.
18Do we get a certificate or logo when we become a Solutions Partner?
Yes. Digital badge/logo for each solution area designation earned. Common benefits include GTM services, TP&D advisory hours, and technical support incidents. Download badge graphics from Partner Center.
19We are a new partner with no prior customers. How should we approach earning a designation?
  • Focus on Skilling first — most directly controllable category
  • Use Microsoft Learn (free), Partner Training Center, and Partner Skilling Hub
  • Plan for initial customer engagements (even small POCs) and use PAL/CPOR from day one
  • Set realistic goals: 6–12+ months; use Partner Center dashboard to track what you need

Certifications & Skilling — Q&A
11 Q&A
01Why are Microsoft certifications important in the partner program?
Certifications are directly tied to the Skilling category of your PCS. Without certifications, you cannot earn Skilling points, and you need >0 in every category to qualify for a designation.
02How do we find out which certifications we need?
(1) Partner Center Solutions Partner Dashboard — live scores, Skilling breakdowns and gaps; (2) Microsoft Learn Partner Center Documentation — official metric definitions and certification lists per solution area.
03How can our team prepare for certification exams?
  • Microsoft Learn — free online learning platform
  • Instructor-Led Training — Microsoft authorized learning partners
  • Practice Exams — from authorized providers
  • Microsoft Docs — exam skills outline on each certification exam's page
04How do we make sure our employees' certifications count toward our partner account?
Each certified individual must be associated with your org in Partner Center (role: Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admin). Employees' MCID must be linked to your Partner Center organization. Verify via the Solutions Partner dashboard.
05Do Microsoft certifications expire?
Yes. Many role-based certifications expire (typically after one year for associate certs). Microsoft provides free renewal assessments online. The partner program counts only active certifications.
06What's the difference between "intermediate" and "advanced" certifications in partner scoring?
  • Intermediate (Associate level) — Mid-level certifications demonstrating foundational expertise
  • Advanced (Expert or Specialty) — Higher-tier certifications indicating deeper expertise
For Infrastructure: 5 employees with qualifying intermediate certs earns 20 points; qualifying advanced certs earn another 20 points.
07Do Microsoft Learn "Fundamentals" certifications (like AZ-900) count for partner skilling points?
  • MS-900 (M365 Fundamentals) DOES count — eligible intermediate cert for Modern Work
  • AZ-900 does NOT count — not listed for any Azure area
Bottom line: MS-900 specifically counts for Modern Work only.
08We have a new employee who passed Microsoft exams – how do we add them?
1. Add them to Partner Center as a user (Account settings → Users → send invitation)
2. Employee logs into Microsoft Learn certification profile → Settings → "Associate to Partner Organization" → enter your Partner ID
3. After associating, certifications will be linked (may take a short while).
09If a certified employee leaves the company, do we lose the certification points?
Yes. If a certified individual leaves and is no longer associated, their certifications will stop counting. Try to have multiple people with required certifications.
10Do certifications count for multiple solution areas or just one?
Some certifications count toward multiple areas. Azure Administrator Associate and Azure Solutions Architect Expert are prerequisites across all three Azure designations. Domain-specific certs (e.g., Dynamics 365 Functional Consultant) only count toward Business Applications.
11Which certifications count toward Skilling points (by Solution Area)?
See the full list below in the "Certification Lists by Area" section.

F
Partner Association (PAL, CPOR, DPOR)
12 Q&A
01If we do work for a customer, how do we get Microsoft to recognize it?
  • PAL (Partner Admin Link) — For Azure plan subscriptions (modern commerce)
  • DPOR (Digital Partner of Record) — For Enterprise Agreement (EA) subscriptions. Does not transfer when customer moves from EA to modern commerce.
  • CPOR (Claiming Partner of Record) — For Microsoft 365 and Dynamics 365 workloads. Customer must approve the association.
  • CSP — When you are the transacting reseller. Automatically tied to your partner ID.
02What exactly is Partner Admin Link (PAL)?
PAL allows a partner's individual (who has access to a customer's Azure environment) to associate their Partner ID with that access. Once established, the customer's ACR and deployments count toward the partner's PCS. Setup requires access via: Guest user, Directory account, Service principal, or Azure Lighthouse.
03How do we set up PAL in practice?
1. Partner employee must have RBAC access to customer's Azure subscription
2. In Azure Portal → account settings → enter org's Partner ID under "Partner Admin Link"
3. Association is recorded in Microsoft's systems
4. Verify in Partner Center within the next scoring cycle. Repeat for each team member with Azure access.
04What is CPOR (Claiming Partner of Record)?
Used for Microsoft 365 and Dynamics 365 workloads. Allows a partner to claim credit for influencing a customer's adoption, even when not the transacting reseller. Eligible for: Modern Work (Enterprise + DPOR, SMB + CSP) and Security (M365 security workloads). Customer must approve.
05When should we use CPOR vs. PAL vs. being a reseller?
  • PAL — Azure subscriptions under modern commerce when not the reseller
  • CPOR — Microsoft 365 or Dynamics 365 projects where you are not the license seller
  • CSP/Reseller — If you sold the subscription; automatically tied to your partner ID
  • DPOR — Customers on Enterprise Agreements (EA); does NOT transfer when customer moves to modern commerce
06We provide services but the customer bought licenses from another partner – can we still get credit?
Yes. Use the appropriate association method: Azure → PAL; Microsoft 365/Dynamics → CPOR. Microsoft's systems will recognize your influence even though billing went through another partner.
07Does PAL or CPOR give us extra permissions or access to the customer's environment?
No. PAL and CPOR are for recognition and tracking only. PAL does not grant access — you already need access before you can link your ID. CPOR doesn't change any permissions in the customer's tenant.
08Will customers know if we associate via PAL or CPOR?
For PAL: customer may not notice unless they look at Azure portal settings. For CPOR: customer must approve the association — Microsoft sends them a notification. Always talk to the customer beforehand.
09What if a customer has multiple partners – who gets the credit?
Multiple associations are possible. For Azure (PAL), multiple partner IDs can be linked to the same subscription via different user accounts. Focus on ensuring your own association is in place.
10We set up PAL/CPOR but still don't see any points – what could be wrong?
  • Timing — PAL attributions may take a billing cycle; CPOR claims also need time after approval
  • Incorrect Partner ID — Use the Associated PartnerID shown on your partner profile
  • Scope of association — For PAL, use subscription-level access for full credit
  • Eligible workloads — For Azure Infrastructure, all services are eligible except virtual machines and VM licenses
11We heard about "GDAP" – is that related to partner recognition?
GDAP (Granular Delegated Admin Privileges) is about operational access — not recognition. GDAP = operational access; PAL/CPOR = attribution and recognition.
12Are there tools to track customer deployments or usage for partner metrics?
Partner Center is your primary tool. The Reports/Insights section shows Azure consumption by customer, CPOR claim statuses, etc. Official metrics only appear once associations are in place.

Partner Benefits — Q&A
7 Q&A
01What are "partner benefits" in the program?
Resources and perks Microsoft provides: Product licenses for internal use · Technical support (advisory hours and support incidents) · GTM resources (marketing materials, partner directory listing) · Training and enablement (workshops, community access).
02What are partner incentives?
Financial rewards (rebates or payments) for driving sales or usage of Microsoft products. Microsoft's primary incentive vehicle is the Microsoft Commerce Incentives (MCI) program.
03How are incentives different from benefits?
Benefits = resources, tools, licenses, support provided as part of your membership. Pre-packaged.
Incentives = cash payouts, rebates earned by performing eligible activities. Based on measured performance.
04What is the Microsoft Action Pack?
The Microsoft Action Pack Subscription (MAPS) — an entry-level paid offering providing: internal use licenses (e.g., M365 seats, some Azure credit), a limited number of partner support incidents, and access to training and marketing resources.
05What are the new "partner benefits packages"?
Microsoft introduced tiered benefits packages ranging from entry-level (Action Pack equivalent) to more advanced packages for Solutions Partners. Three cost-saving packages are available on the Partner Center page.
06Do we get any benefits immediately after joining?
With just a free membership: Access to Partner Center tools and community · Access to the Training Center · Ability to buy an Action Pack · Eligibility to pursue designations. You won't automatically get internal use licenses or credits for free.
07What benefits do we get once we become a Solutions Partner?
Enhanced benefits (2026): $10,000/year Azure bulk production credits · 25 Visual Studio Enterprise IDE subscriptions · GitHub Copilot Enterprise $5,000/year · Microsoft Dragon Copilot 20 users · Partner Marketing Center Pro (Unlimited) · 50 partner cloud support incidents · 20 Microsoft Product Support incidents · GTM services · TP&D advisory hours.

Specializations
10 Q&A
01What is the difference between a Solutions Partner designation and a Specialization?
A designation is the foundational achievement in a broad solution area. A Specialization is a step above — demonstrating deep, specific expertise in a narrower domain. Prerequisite: must already hold the relevant Solutions Partner designation. No separate cost. Many specializations require passing an audit.
02How many specializations does Microsoft offer?
Azure (13+): AI Platform, Analytics, AI Apps, Data Warehouse Migration, Agentic DevOps, Hybrid Cloud HCI, Infra and Database Migration, Kubernetes, Azure Virtual Desktop, VMware Solution, Migrate Enterprise Applications, Networking Services, SAP on Azure.
Business Applications (8): Business Intelligence, Finance, Intelligent Automation, Low Code App Dev, Sales, Service, SMB Management, Supply Chain.
Modern Work (7): Adoption & Change Management, Calling for Teams, Custom Solutions for Teams, Meetings & Meeting Rooms, Modernize Endpoints, Teamwork Deployment, Copilot.
Security (5): Cloud Security, Identity & Access Management, Data Security, Threat Protection, Copilot.
03What are the requirements to achieve a Specialization?
For Azure specializations: must meet qualification requirements (aligned Solutions Partner designation, Performance and Skilling requirements), then schedule and pass the audit. For Modern Work and Security: different enrollment path.
04How do we prepare for a specialization audit?
  • Review the requirements document in detail (Partner Center Specializations FAQ)
  • Organize documentation: architecture diagrams, project scopes, customer attestations
  • Perform a self-assessment or dry run
  • Ensure your team is ready — auditors might interview technical team members
05Do specializations require an on-site audit or is it remote?
In most cases, audits are done remotely by third-party auditors — they schedule calls to review documentation and ask questions. You typically upload documentation to a secure portal, followed by interviews.
06How long does a Specialization last?
Generally valid for one year. You must keep your base Solutions Partner designation active — losing the underlying designation means losing the specialization.
07What is the Azure Expert MSP program?
The Azure Expert Managed Service Provider (MSP) program is a top-tier distinction for partners providing end-to-end Azure managed services. Requires an intensive audit covering people, processes, and Azure managed service capabilities. Only a small number of partners globally hold this status.
08Are specializations mandatory?
Completely optional. Not required to be a Microsoft partner and not required for most incentive programs. They are an added mark of distinction.
09Do we have to pay for a specialization?
No separate fee is required for specializations if you're enrolled in the Solution Partner designation program. However, you must maintain your underlying Solutions Partner benefits package subscription.
10Will Microsoft provide a badge for specializations?
Yes. Microsoft provides a digital Specialization badge/logo as an add-on to your Solutions Partner badge. Your partner profile will display both the Solutions Partner designation and the specialization credential.

Incentives & Rewards
6 Q&A
01How do we become eligible for Microsoft partner incentives?
1. Achieve a Solutions Partner designation (at least one) as a baseline requirement
2. Enroll in and sign the incentives agreement in Partner Center
3. Meet specific program requirements for each incentive area. Microsoft's Microsoft Commerce Incentives (MCI) program covers claiming scenarios and proof of execution (POE) guidelines.
02What types of incentives can partners expect?
  • Azure consumption incentives — based on ACR driven through PAL or CSP
  • Modern Work incentives — based on selling or influencing M365 seats and active usage
  • Business Applications incentives — for selling and driving adoption of Dynamics 365 and Power Platform
  • Program-specific funding — ECIF, AMP, and PIE increasingly tied to Advanced Specializations
03How are incentives paid out?
Incentives are typically paid out quarterly or monthly depending on the program. You must set up payment and tax details in Partner Center.
04We are not transacting any deals (not selling directly). Can we still earn incentives?
Yes, in many cases. For Azure, if you have a PAL association and drive usage up, you may qualify for influence incentives. The key is having the corresponding Solutions Partner designation and enrolling in the incentives program.
05Are there any incentives for referring customers if we don't do the work ourselves?
The partner program has largely moved away from pure referral fees. The old "Advisor" model has been retired. Current incentive models encourage partners to be involved in implementation/adoption (using CPOR/PAL) or to resell via CSP.
06What's the difference between being a partner in MCPP vs being a distributor or LSP?
A Distributor (Indirect Provider) wholesales Microsoft products to other partners — very different track, high requirements. An LSP sells volume licenses under enterprise agreements. Unless your model is recruiting other resellers, you are likely a standard partner or CSP reseller.

Co-sell, Marketplace & GTM
12 Q&A
01What does "co-selling with Microsoft" mean?
Collaborating with Microsoft's sales teams (or other partners) to sell solutions to customers. Forms: Microsoft field-led (a Microsoft salesperson brings you in) · Partner-led (you have a solution Microsoft promotes) · Partner-to-partner (two partners team up).
02What is the Microsoft commercial marketplace?
  • Azure Marketplace — IT solutions (Azure VM solutions, SaaS apps, developer and data services)
  • Microsoft AppSource — Business users (Dynamics 365 add-ons, Power BI visuals, SaaS, consulting services)
  • Listing is free; transactable offers incur a standard marketplace transaction fee
03How do we list our solution or service on the marketplace?
In Partner Center, go to the Marketplace section → create a Publisher profile → create a New Offer → provide descriptions, pricing, and other details. Microsoft reviews the offer before it goes live. Start with a simple "Contact me" listing if you just want lead generation.
04What go-to-market (GTM) support does Microsoft provide?
  • Partner Marketing Center — ready-made marketing materials, campaign content, AI-powered customization
  • Solutions Partner listing — customers find you via Microsoft partner directory, filtered by designations and specializations
  • Co-branded materials and logo usage guidelines
  • Events and webinars where partners can be featured
05What is the Microsoft partner directory and how do we appear in it?
Microsoft maintains a Partner Finder where customers search for partners by expertise and location. Ensure your business profile in Partner Center is complete. Solutions Partner designations and specializations automatically display on your profile and influence search rankings.
06What is the Microsoft Partner Community?
An online forum (microsoftpartnercommunity.com) where partners ask questions, share experiences, and get updates from Microsoft and other partners. Free, available to all partners.
07What support can we expect as a new partner?
Partner Center Support (file support tickets) · Technical Pre-sales & Deployment (TP&D) advisory hours · Partner Support Communities · Microsoft Learn (free learning paths) · MCAPS Start for Partners resource portal.
08Can we use the old "Gold Partner" logo?
No. The old Gold and Silver logos are no longer valid. Partners lost associated badging in October 2022. Use only the current Solutions Partner badges.
09We thought we'd automatically get leads and incentives when we joined – is that true?
No. Simply joining the program does not guarantee leads or payments. Leads come from marketing efforts, co-sell programs, and the partner directory. Incentives require achieving at least one Solutions Partner designation, enrolling in an incentives program, and performing qualifying activities.
10We have lots of certifications but no customer activity – will we still get a designation?
Unlikely. You need at least some points in every category. Even if you max out Skilling, you still need some new customers (Performance) and some deployments/usage (Customer Success) to reach the 70-point threshold.
11Our customer moved from an Enterprise Agreement to an Azure plan – does our partner association carry over?
No. DPOR status does not move with them. The partner ID is not transferred. You need to set up PAL for the new subscription.
12What if we can't reach 70 points – are there any benefits for partners below the threshold?
You can still purchase an Action Pack for basic benefits regardless of your score. Some general partner resources are available to all enrolled partners. However, designation-level benefits require meeting the 70-point threshold.

Partner Center Basics
8 Q&A
01How do I access Partner Center?
Go to partner.microsoft.com and click on Partner Center. Sign in with the Microsoft account associated with your partner enrollment.
02What roles exist in Partner Center and who can do what?
Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admin — manages partner capability scores and membership activities. Account admin — linking partner IDs via PAL/DPOR. Other roles exist for billing, referrals, incentives management, etc.
03Where in Partner Center can I see our partner capability score?
Navigate to the Membership section. You'll find enrollment status, qualification status, and metric-level details for each solution area you're tracking.
04Where do I manage partner associations (PAL, CPOR)?
PAL is set up in the Azure Portal (not Partner Center) → "Link to a partner ID" page. CPOR claims are managed in Partner Center under the Incentives or Associations section.
05Where do I see our benefits and download software?
In Partner Center, navigate to the Benefits section. There you can find and activate product licenses, download software, manage Azure credits, and access support incidents.
06What if I have technical issues with Partner Center?
Use the Support section within Partner Center to file a support request. Check Partner Center guidance on Microsoft Learn. Microsoft has regional support centers for partners.
07How do I add or remove users from my Partner Center account?
An admin manages users under Account settings in Partner Center. To add users: send an invitation via their email. To remove users: find them in the user list and disable or remove their access. Note: removing a certified user means their certifications stop counting toward your Skilling points.
08How do I update my company profile in the partner directory?
In Partner Center, go to Account settings → Business profile and update your company description, industries, solutions, location, and contact information.

M
Common Mistakes & Misunderstandings
1
Not setting up partner associations (PAL/CPOR). The most costly mistake. Without associations, partner capability score points are not recognized. Every customer engagement must be linked to your Partner ID.
2
Confusing benefits with incentives. Benefits are resources provided as part of your membership. Incentives are cash payouts earned through performance. New partners sometimes assume they'll receive cash payments just by joining.
3
Assuming designation happens automatically upon hitting 70 points. For new partners not previously enrolled, you may need to actively purchase the Solutions Partner designation on the Membership offers page once you qualify.
4
Relying on a single certified employee. If that person leaves, you lose Skilling points. Microsoft's scoring requires multiple distinct individuals with certifications.
5
Not renewing certifications. Expired certifications stop counting. Your Skilling score can drop suddenly if renewals are missed.
6
Using the wrong Partner ID for PAL. Microsoft specifies using the Associated PartnerID value shown on your partner profile. Using a different ID (global account vs. location account) can cause attribution issues.
7
Assuming DPOR transfers when a customer migrates from EA to Azure plan. The partner ID is not transferred. You need to set up PAL for the new subscription.
8
Thinking Gold/Silver competencies still exist. No more legacy competency renewals have been processed since October 2022.
9
Not completing the business verification during enrollment. Microsoft will verify email ownership, employment, and other details — you must follow time-sensitive instructions.
10
Ignoring the SMB path. Partners serving small businesses may qualify via the SMB track with lower thresholds but may not realize this option exists.
